![[kawi1.webp|BMW M2 on Mount Magazine]]

The Kawasaki KE100 was an entry level dualsport that saw its introduction in 1976 and continued until 2001, becoming the last street legal two stroke motorcycle for sale in the US. At 11 horsepower and a price equivalent to a modern Honda Navi the 100cc mosquito repulsion device was no competition bike but for many a 16 year old it was their first street-legal introduction to two wheels and, as Kawasaki's advertisements put it, it sure beat a moped.

![[kawi2.webp|Pasted image 20260811142833.png]]

I picked this gem up from a local guy who bought it to re-live his youth, but his lack of mechanical ability combined with the bike sitting more than he would like led to it getting listed for $950 "needs a battery and the carb cleaned". Sure.

The bike ran, but only with the choke on; a sure sign of an air leak somewhere. A night of disassembly resulted in the engine blown apart across half of my buddy's shop as we got increasingly baffled as the number of potential leak areas dwindled. Eventually I leaned over and puffed air through the intake tube and was rewarded with a blast of air back into my eyes from the base of the cylinder. Base gasket leak? The reason was obvious when I went to loosen the head nuts and was greeted with a 1/4" socket stuck to one of the nuts, and the force required to loosen all four bolts was doable with a 1/4" micro-ratchet gently clasped between thumb and forefinger. "To spec", indeed.

![[kawi3.webp]]

A gasket kit, a new plug, and the carb spending some time in the ultrasonic cleaner and the bike went back together just as neat as anything. An inspection of the crankshaft and the cylinder liner (nikasil; so fancy!) when the jug was off neatly corroborated the sub-5,000 mile claim on the instrument cluster. Two kicks and the bike sprang to life and settled into a chainsaw-like idle as every head nearby shot up wondering who was having a tree removed and I putted around the neighborhood.

Since then the bike has been excellent; that first night it saw both street and trail and the 200lb soaking wet weight and low seat height makes it an absolute blast on single-track, and the 2-stroke power delivery is not as notable as actual performance machines but the complete and utter lack of torque still demanded a very different approach to keeping the bike "on the pipe" and in the powerband to maintain momentum. Hill starts? Perilous.

Since then I have remedied a couple more issues; a new petcock that *almost* fits correctly was installed and the old ball bearing steering stem bearings were replaced with a set of tapered roller bearings from Allballs to resolve a "notchy" feeling in the steering that I believe was caused by contaminated, dried grease.

![[kawi4.webp]]

Coming up next will be a new set of brake shoes, some nice fresh tires (white letter, perhaps?), and finding a way to shore up the 6 volt electrical system so the headlight has more luminosity than a candle on a foggy evening.

I have a lot of bikes in my fleet right now. A fire-breathing titanium-exhausted 1,000cc Italian Aprilia that just received a Gabro tune, a weird Vectrix VX-1 electric maxi-scooter that sounds like a spaceship and can hold my groceries, a Honda Magna V-4 with the baffles drilled out that sounds like a thousand demons chasing me down the road, and a Kawasaki KLX300 dualsport that is superior to the KE in every single respect. Despite all of this I find myself reaching over and over again for the keys to the floppy-chassis, useless suspension, 11 horsepower, 55mph top speed little enduro from the 70s. It is, in almost all respects, a terrible motorcycle. But when I kick it over with what feels like not enough effort and it dutifully springs to life into a _ting ting ting ting_ idle there is one thing that it does better than almost anything else:

it makes me smile.
