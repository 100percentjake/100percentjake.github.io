About a year ago I surveyed my fleet of misshapen shitboxes and realized the economic value could amount to something greater. Something great, even. My typical inability to be patient once I have mentally committed to something kicked in and instead of selling cars first and buying their replacement second I, of course, got a loan so I could have the new shiny *right now* while listing my E39 M5, C5 Corvette, and NC Miata for sale. But what would replace this diverse fleet of hoopties?

A 2020 BMW M2 Competition

## The end.

![[img/m2_magazine.jpg|BMW M2 on Mount Magazine]]

My desire for an M2 did not originate when the M2 was released. It was another pretty obvious progression of schmedium-small sized BMW sports coupes, most directly following the lineage of the E46 M3 which it is the most similar to in dimensions. Take the formula, add power. Nothing groundbreaking but notably I preferred its styling to its "this should be a 5 series" sized F80 M3 brethren, whose headlights possessed "tear ducts" connecting them to the kidney grilles, a styling feature I found personally repulsive.

Nonetheless, the M2 resided as a footnote in BMWs lineup. Too modern and expensive to be in my realm of possibility at the time, and I assumed that whatever would come next would be even better. Then the G87 M2 came out and proved me wrong.

I'm not going to be a rose tinted glasses fanboy and even remotely imply the F87 M2 is small. It's not. It's a 2 series the size of an E46 M3, which at the time was considered an absolute porker weighing in at 300lbs more than the non-M variants (option-dependent), but the size of these two cars feels "right" for this segment. With the release of the G87 it is clear that BMW no longer has any intentions of catering to this segment and retaining a car in this particular size class. On top of this, the interior got significantly more "boy racer", beautiful physical gauges went away in lieu of a gaming-PC styled bar graph screen interface, the seats got... idk, thigh dividers? And the best looking steering wheel BMW has ever made was thrown out in favor of something that looks like a mild update of the atrocity that was in the BMW i3 and i8.

Needless to say, I was so unimpressed I was suddenly pushed to buy an F87 M2 while they were still a new-ish car, before the TikTok crowd had their way with them and reduced the number of clean examples into the single digits fetching six figures on BringATrailerFullOfCash, so my collection was liquified and a suitable candidate was purchased.

## The car.

![[img/m2_dragon.jpg|BMW M2 on the Tail of the Dragon]]

[Extremely James May voice] And here's what I've bought. A 2020 M2 Competition in Hockenheim Silver with every option that could be checked with the critical exclusion of an automatic transmission and a sunroof. The Hockenheim Silver is a ridiculously cool silver that flips from blueish silver to gold in the sunlight, but I would have preferred an actual color. C'est la vie.

Coming from my previous daily driver 2008 328i the most immediate impressions of the F87 were *wow this car is crazy fast* and *wow this car is built like shit*. I am aware that the 2 series is meant to be a ladder rung below the 3 series, but I did not expect such a hit to the interior build quality. A 17,000 mile car should not be in the same league as a 140,000 mile car for interior squeaks and rattles but the F87 provides a symphony of, ah, *character* to keep the driver entertained over anything but the smoothest of roads. Fortunately for the integrity of the interior plastics the suspension on the M2 is not of the "we made it sporty by making it harsh and crashy" variety, as has been the case in so many cars I have owned in the past. The ride on a smooth highway is fantastic, and the ride on a crap highway is tolerable. What more could one ask for? The active shocks of the M2CS might be an improvement, Bilstein or Koni may have offerings that provide a mild improvement but $2500 for a set of Bilstein B16s I'm happy to leave that experience to my imagination. Maybe 60k miles down the line when the factory Sachs have met their maker I'll drop the coin, but for now I find it unjustifiable. 

As far as non-factory hardware my M2 had everything I'd want and nothing I wouldn't. The S55 is somewhat notorious in the BMW community for having a horrid exhaust note, which I disagreed heavily with until realizing that the exhaust on this car had been modified with straight pipes where the center resonators were. This results in a hell of a scream in "Sport" mode with the valves open and dead silence in "Comfort", just as I'd like it. If anything I'd prefer that it be slightly louder in Sport mode but I wouldn't want to compromise the highway comfort of having no audible exhaust whatsoever. The exhaust isn't a solo player, however, and is joined in a beautiful duet by the turbos screaming their (dis?)pleasure through a pair of aftermarket cone air filter intake... thingies. I'm sure the performance improvement is completely nonexistent but the experience is vastly improved. All manner of whistling, chuffing, chattering, and blowoff type noises come from the front of the car as you row through the gears and it is ENDLESSLY entertaining. 

Other aftermarket parts include a lip spoiler on the trunk, a front lip, some coding changes (full screen iDrive, auto comfort entry seats, auto start/stop disabled, ASD disabled, etc.) and the biggest change: M2CS software. The M2CS and a fully loaded M2C are pretty similar cars. The same engine, same steel brakes, same transmission, I think the main difference are the CF roof and the active shocks. As such, it's possible to load the entire software config from the CS onto the Comp and gain its improvements including a bump of 35+ horsepower, a "spicier" traction control and stability control tune and sportier electronic power steering. This is a must-have "mod" to your M2C as you get the reliability of it being "OEM" and you get, well, all the benefits I mentioned above. One of the biggest benefits is that the factory "burble tune" that is active in Sport/Sport+ mode is toned down from "ridiculous" to "amusing". It is possible to turn it off with BimmerLink if so inclined.

The one *downgrade* on my car is that the stock Pilot SuperSport summer tires have been replaced with Continental ContiProContact... DWS. Yeah, all-seasons. Unfortunately with how fresh they are I can't in good faith justify replacing nearly-new tires. It will get some Falken summer tires eventually, ideally they would be paired with a set of forged F80 M3 wheels because the stock wheels on the M2 are HEAVY. I don't know what BMWs malfunction is with putting boat anchors on all four corners of their "sporty" cars but it seems to be yet another train inherited from the E46, where the M3 and ZHP got the heaviest wheels out of the whole lineup.

## The Drive.

![[img/m2_miles.jpg|BMW M2 as it hit 30,000 miles]]

I've had the pleasure of driving my M2 on a variety of truly excellent roads as well as some (wet) track time at Hallett Motorsports Park, and so far the place that the car has felt the most at home has been Tail of the Dragon. This might come as a surprise that the M2 would feel at home at a tight, twisty road more suited to Miatas and Lotuses, but it was one of the only places where I felt like I could actually USE the car. Where I live, in Wichita, there aren't many opportunities to use the car as it was intended, instead relegated to quick blasts to 60 on on-ramps. This means the fun is limited to less than 4 seconds. At the Tail the fun just doesn't end. The road is too tight for the car to get into the sort of speeds where a mistake means a body bad, so you can just flog with abandon ensuring you leave enough margin between yourself and the funny yellow line in the center of the road. The front/rear balance is perfect, as is usually the case with BMWs of this size. On the all-seasons the limit is extremely progressive and pronounced making it very easy to play around and experiment with what the car does as it loses traction. On the stock staggered tire and wheel package the car simply "crabs" sideways evenly as the limit is approached until the stability control intervenes and does *something* that makes the car just... slew around to where the steering wheel is pointing. I'm not sure what sort of magic wizards Bosch employs but their stability control is called "hand of God" for a very good reason. Turn the stability control to "M Dynamic Mode" and suddenly the car has an extra 150 horsepower on corner exit and the air gains a faint smell of burnt rubber as the car stops pulling power as quickly when tire slip is sensed. Turn traction control off all the way and the car becomes a steer-with-throttle weapon (in an empty parking lot, of course) with telepathic control of the 3,500 pounds of metal surrounding you. The traction at the front and rear tires is communicated directly through the seat and up your spine and even the electronic power steering allows you to tell the exactly how much grip the front tires have left. You can't tell if the coin you just ran over is a nickel or a dime, such is the nature of the beast, but at least you know it's there. As someone with the misfortune of driving BMWs early forays into EPS with the E85/E86 Z4 non-M, it's truly amazing how much improvement they were able to bring to the table in one generation of product.

If the chassis is the star of the show the engine is the light and sound system, because it makes the whole experience even better and I have to give massive kudos to BMW for avoiding the horrific tendency of the automotive industry to "NA-ify" their turbo engines. Minimize boost lag, smooth out the power curve, try to inject as much torque as possible. *BOOOORING*. The S55 with the M2CS tune is *unhinged* and I absolutely love it. Leaving a stop light in this car results in a modest amount of acceleration until the very second that the twin turbochargers light off and the car promptly performs a rolling burnout as the horsepower seemingly doubles. Add to this the fact that the driving modes in the M2 *actually change things*. Throttle map, sure, traction control, sure, but the S55 has electronically actuated wastegates which means the ECU has direct control over how much boost the car is making at all times (assuming enough exhaust velocity is present). This means that in "economy" mode the car is effectively naturally aspirated until you floor it, at which point the car will seem to pause for a moment before finally allowing you to have a moderate amount of boost for passing on the highway. Hit the button for Sport+ and *everything* changes. The throttle gets more sensitive, the exhaust valves open up, the turbos snarl at the slightest twitch of your right foot. It is simply addictive.

## Living with it.

![[img/m2_int.jpg|BMW M2 interior]]

All of this said, how well does the M2, y'know, car? It rides nice and drives wonderful, but that doesn't necessarily mean a car is usable day to day. My biggest takeaway is: if daily driving is more than 70% of your use case for the car you're probably better off with an M4. The larger size and nicer interior with more amenities makes for a great GT car that's a bit closer to a Mustang in size and, well, there's a very good reason so many people are daily driving their Mustangs. This said, the M2 is usable in a pinch. The back seat, once you squeeze past the front seats, is shocking roomy and the trunk borders on cavernous especially when combined with quickly folding rear seats. For me as a weekend/road trip car, it is ideal. 

Even the fuel economy isn't a massive ding against the M2. On road trips it is not uncommon for me to get 23mpg on the highway and 15-17mpg when the car has been beaten on, which you will be encouraged to do because this car seems to attract an inordinate amount of attention from brainlets on the interstate who want nothing more in life than to roll-race in traffic. I recommend drowning out their inevitably horrific exhausts with the Harmon/Kardon sound system which I would describe as... serviceable. I bolstered mine with a 300w Alpine amplifier and a custom-fit low profile trunk enclosure with a JL Audio low-profile 10" subwoofer and the result is about everything I'd want from a budget audio system in an occasional use car.

## Conclusion

![[img/m2_woods.jpg|BMW M2 on in the woods]]

Every time BMW comes out with a new generation of 3 series it seems like it's "The last". The E46 M3 was the last NA I6, the E90 M3 was the last NA analog hydraulic power steering M car, the F80 is the last that had analog gauges, the G80 is the last that will be a manual. I think the M2 is the last iteration of the BMW E30. The last upright small (relatively) coupe with a simple RWD manual, drive engagement as a focus, and what I think is a goldilocks zone of amenities and technology in the interior that I sincerely believe will age better than what came before (no Bluetooth audio streaming/carplay) or what has come after (massive wraparound tablet dashboard with trendy digital gauges).

I think the M2 as a whole will not age. A future collectible, though not an investment. The proliferation of TikTok "builds" has hit the M2 community less so than the M3 and M4 communities but I don't expect the survival numbers of clean M2s to be particularly rosy. I won't be preserving mine, it'll get driven. It's nothing special in the grand scheme of things, just a car, but it is a very special car.

Hopefully that makes sense.
